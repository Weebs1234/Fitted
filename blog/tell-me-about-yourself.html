"Tell me about yourself": a simple answer that works in any interview

The first interview question is the one people prepare least. A three-part answer of about a minute, with examples, and the mistakes to avoid.

"So, tell me a bit about yourself." It's the first question in almost every interview, and the one most people improvise. That's a shame, because it sets the tone for everything after it.

The good news: you only need to prepare it once, then adjust it a little for each job.

What they're really asking

They're not asking for your life story. They want to know three things: who you are professionally, whether you fit this role, and whether you can explain yourself clearly. Aim for about one minute.

Use three parts: now, before, next

1. Now (who you are today). Your current or most recent role, and what you're good at.

"I'm an operations manager. For the last few years I've run logistics and customer service for a European team of about 40 people."

2. Before (how you got here). One or two highlights from earlier jobs that matter for this role. Pick the ones that match the job ad.

"Before that I led technical support at a consumer electronics brand, where I improved how we handled repairs and returns."

3. Next (why this job). Why you're here, linked to something specific in the ad.

"Now I'm looking for a role where I can build a team from the start again, and your plan to open a second warehouse is exactly that."

That's it. Three short parts, then let them ask follow-up questions.

Make it fit each job

Keep "now" mostly the same. Change "before" and "next" for each application: pick the highlight that matches what the ad asks for, and name something from the ad in your last sentence. It shows you read it, and it steers the conversation to your strengths.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Starting with where you were born or went to school. Unless it's relevant, start with your work.
  • Reading out your CV. They have it. Pick highlights instead.
  • Going on for five minutes. If you see them waiting to ask something, wrap up.
  • Being negative about your last employer. Even if it was bad, keep it neutral: "I'm ready for a new challenge."
  • Saying things you can't back up. Every claim you make here can come back as a question.

Practise it out loud

Say it out loud three times, or record it on your phone. You'll hear where it drags. You don't need to learn it word for word, just the three parts and the one highlight per part.
