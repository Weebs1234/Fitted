How to tailor your CV to a job ad (without making anything up)

Tailor your CV to a job ad in fifteen minutes. Which lines to change, where to find the words, and what not to do.

You send the same CV to ten jobs and hear nothing back. Sound familiar? A recruiter first looks at your job titles, your employers and whether the words from the ad come back. That takes seconds. If nothing matches, you're on to the next pile.

The good news: you don't need a new CV for every job. A few lines in the right place make the difference.

Why tailoring works

In a study of almost 2 million applications, CVs tailored to the job ad were invited to interview 1.85 times as often as untailored ones (Huntr, Job Search Trends Q2 2026). That shows a link, not a promise. But it fits what recruiters say themselves: they look for proof you can do this exact job.

Step 1: pull the requirements out of the ad

Read the ad and write down the three to five things that really matter. Look for words that come back, and for the requirements at the top or in a "what we ask" list. That's what a recruiter checks.

Example for a warehouse team lead:

  • leading a team
  • shift planning
  • safety on the floor

Step 2: find something you really did for each one

Go through your own experience. Where did you do something that fits? Think concrete: how big was the team, what did you plan, what got better?

It has to be true. Anything on your CV can come up in an interview. If you can't explain it with an example, leave it out.

Step 3: rewrite the first line of each job

The first line under a job gets read the most. Make it the line that fits the ad, in the ad's own words.

  • Before: Responsible for daily warehouse operations.
  • After: Planned rosters for two shifts of 40 people and kept the floor fully staffed through peak season.

Same job, same facts. Now the top line is what this employer is looking for.

Step 4: check your profile and skills

Do you have a short profile at the top? Let it say in two or three sentences who you are for this role. And put your skills in the order the ad asks for: what they care about most comes first.

What not to do

  • Don't exaggerate. Don't write "led" if you helped. Recruiters ask follow-up questions.
  • No keywords without substance. A list of loose words convinces nobody. Show what you did with that skill.
  • Don't rewrite everything. A few good lines are enough. The rest of your CV can stay as it is.
