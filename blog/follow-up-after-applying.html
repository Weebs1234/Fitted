Following up after a job application: when and how

How long to wait before you follow up, what to put in your message, and what to do when you hear nothing. With an example you can use straight away.

You applied and it's gone quiet. Following up may feel pushy, but a short, friendly message is usually welcome. It shows you're really interested, and sometimes it puts your application back on top of the pile.

When to follow up

  • The ad gives a date, or the recruiter said when you'd hear? Wait until that date has passed, plus a few working days.
  • No date given? A week to ten days after applying is reasonable.
  • After an interview: send a short thank-you the same or next day. If you then hear nothing by the agreed time, follow up.

How to follow up

Short, personal and specific. Mention the role, when you applied and one thing that appeals to you. No complaints about how long it's taking.

Dear Jan,

On 28 October I applied for the Customer Service Supervisor role. I'm still very interested, especially in how you organise the team around peak times. Is there any news about the next step?

Kind regards, Sanne

Send it to the contact person in the ad, or the recruiter you were in touch with. On LinkedIn it can be even shorter.

And if you still hear nothing?

Following up once is enough. If another week passes with no answer, move on to other applications. That's not a failure: a lot of companies simply never reply.

Keep track of where you stand

With several applications going at once, it's easy to lose track. Note for each job when you applied, who you spoke to and when you'll follow up.
