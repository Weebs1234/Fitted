You've probably heard it: "75% of CVs are rejected by a robot before a human ever reads them." It's one of the most repeated job-search claims online. It's also mostly a myth.

Here's what actually happens to your CV after you click Apply, and what you can do about it.

## What an ATS is

An applicant tracking system (ATS) is the software a company uses to collect and manage applications. Workday, Greenhouse, Recruitee and SmartRecruiters are well-known examples. It stores your CV, lets recruiters search all applicants, and keeps track of who is at which stage.

In most companies, the ATS doesn't decide anything by itself. A recruiter does. But the recruiter uses the ATS to filter, sort and search, and that's where your CV can get lost.

## How CVs really get missed

Three things cause most of the trouble:

1. **Knock-out questions.** "Do you have a driving licence?" or "Can you work in the Netherlands?" If you answer no to a must-have, you can be filtered out automatically. Read these questions carefully.
2. **Search.** A recruiter types "forklift" or "Power BI" and only looks at CVs that contain it. If the word isn't on your CV, you don't show up, even if you have the skill.
3. **Unreadable layouts.** Text inside images, tables or text boxes can come out scrambled when the system reads your CV. The recruiter then sees a mess, or nothing.

## Five things that help

**Use the words from the ad, where they're true.** If the ad asks for "shift planning" and you planned rosters, say "shift planning". Same skill, the words the recruiter will search for.

**Keep the layout simple.** One column, normal headings ("Work experience", "Education"), no text in images. A clean PDF made from text is fine.

**Put your job title and key skills near the top.** A short profile with the role you're applying for and three or four matching skills helps both the software and the person.

**Spell things out once.** Write "Warehouse Management System (WMS)" the first time. Then both versions are on your CV.

**Answer the knock-out questions honestly.** Saying yes to something you can't do will only stop you later in the process.

## What doesn't help

Hiding keywords in white text, copying the whole job ad into your CV, or stuffing your profile with every buzzword. Recruiters see these tricks all the time, and they make you look less trustworthy, not more.

## The short version

An ATS rarely throws your CV away by itself. But a recruiter searching for the right words will miss you if those words aren't there. The fix isn't a trick. It's describing your real experience in the language of the job ad.
