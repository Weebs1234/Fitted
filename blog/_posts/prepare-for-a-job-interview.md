You've been invited. Great! Now the interview itself. The best preparation isn't memorising as many answers as possible, but knowing which examples from your own work you want to tell, and for which question.

## Every round is different

- **Recruiter call:** short and practical. Why this job, what you're looking for, when you can start, your salary expectations. They check whether you broadly fit.
- **First interview:** with the hiring manager. Your experience, how you work, examples of what you've done.
- **Second interview or assessment:** deeper. Difficult situations, how you make decisions, sometimes a task or case.
- **Final interview:** often with someone more senior. Does it fit both ways? Terms and conditions come up here too.

So prepare for the round you're in, not everything at once.

## Step 1: read the job ad again

Take the ad and list the main requirements. Each one probably comes with a question: "Tell me about a time you ..."

## Step 2: choose your examples

Find one real example from your work for each requirement. Tell it briefly in four steps:

1. **Situation:** where were you, what was going on?
2. **Task:** what did you have to do?
3. **Action:** what did you do, concretely?
4. **Result:** what did it achieve? A number helps, if it's true.

> At Noordhaven we were short on people during peak season. I had to keep both shifts staffed. I made a new roster and set up fixed reserves. As a result the floor stayed fully staffed all season.

Four to six good examples are enough. They fit lots of different questions.

## Step 3: practise out loud

Don't just read your examples: say them out loud, or tell them to someone. You'll notice where you go on too long.

## Step 4: prepare your own questions

At the end they almost always ask: "Do you have any questions?" Have two or three ready, about the work itself:

- What does a good first three months look like?
- What's the biggest challenge for the team right now?
- How do you work with other departments?

## After the interview

Send a short thank-you the same or next day. Mention one thing from the conversation that stuck with you. If you hear nothing by the agreed time, follow up politely.
