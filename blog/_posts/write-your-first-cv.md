"I have nothing to put on it." That's what most people say before their first CV, or their first one in years. It's almost never true. You just haven't written it down yet.

## Everything you did counts

A CV isn't only a list of paid jobs. Employers look for proof that you can show up, learn, work with people and get things done. That proof can come from:

- **A side job**: a paper round, a shop, a café, babysitting.
- **An internship or work placement**, even a short one.
- **Volunteering**: the canteen at the sports club, a food bank, a festival.
- **A school project** where you planned, presented or worked in a team.
- **Caring for a family member**: planning, appointments, paperwork, staying calm.
- **A sport or club**: training every week, being captain, organising trips.

If you did it regularly and it took effort, it can go on your CV.

## Ask yourself two questions

For each thing you did, write down the answers to two questions:

1. **What did you do on a normal day?** For example: "Delivered papers to 300 homes before school."
2. **What went well, or what are you proud of?** For example: "Never missed a day, even in winter."

Those two answers are your CV lines. Start each one with a verb: delivered, planned, helped, organised, trained.

## Keep it true and plain

A first CV doesn't need big words. "Helped customers at the till and closed the shop on Saturdays" is better than "Responsible for retail operations". It's clear, it's true, and you can talk about it in an interview.

Don't add numbers or results you can't back up. If you don't know how many customers you served, leave the number out.

## Put it in the right order

1. Your name, town, phone number and email.
2. One or two sentences about who you are and what work you're looking for.
3. What you did, newest first: title, where, when, and your lines.
4. School and courses, newest first.
5. Skills and languages: only what you can really do.

One page is plenty.

## A gap is fine

Been away from work to care for someone, to recover, or to travel? You don't have to explain everything on your CV. A short line is enough, and what you did in that time (caring, volunteering, a course) can go on it like any other experience.
