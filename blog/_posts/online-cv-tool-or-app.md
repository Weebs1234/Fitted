There are more and more tools that use AI to tailor your CV to a job ad. Most work online: you upload your CV to their website and pay per use or per month. There's another approach too: an app on your own computer or phone. What's the difference, and which suits you?

## Where does your CV live?

With an **online tool** you upload your CV to that company's servers. That's convenient: you can log in anywhere. But your CV holds a lot of personal data: your name, address, phone number, your whole work history. So read how long they keep it and what they use it for.

With an **app on your device** your CV stays on your own computer or phone. To draft lines some text does go to an AI service, but you choose which one, and with a local AI nothing leaves your device at all.

## How do you pay?

- **Credits:** you buy a bundle and each action costs credits. Fine if you apply a few times. If you apply a lot, you keep buying more.
- **Subscription:** a fixed amount per month. Handy during an intense search, but remember to cancel once you've found a job.
- **Pay once:** you pay once and keep using the tool. Usually a bit more up front, but no cost per job.

Do the sum. How many jobs do you expect to apply for in the coming months, and what does that cost per job?

## How honest does your CV stay?

Some tools promise a tailored CV in seconds. Fast, but careful: an AI can make things sound bigger than they were, or copy requirements from the ad that you don't have. Anything on your CV can come up in an interview.

So check whether you can review every change before it goes on your CV, and whether the tool only uses facts already on your CV.

## Language and country

Many tools are made for the US market, with a "resume" in English. If you apply in the Netherlands or elsewhere in Europe, you may want a CV in Dutch or another language, sometimes in English as well, and a layout that's usual where you apply.

## More than your CV

An application is more than a CV. Useful to have: a cover letter per job, a list of where you've applied, a reminder to follow up, and interview prep.

## Side by side

| | Online tool | App on your device |
|---|---|---|
| Your CV lives | on their servers | on your own computer or phone |
| Paying | often credits or a subscription | often once |
| Log in anywhere | yes | on the devices you install it on |
| Works without an account | usually not | often yes |

Neither is always better. If you want to try something quickly a few times from any computer, an online tool is handy. If you apply a lot and want to keep your data yourself, an app fits better.
