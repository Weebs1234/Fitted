How to write a cover letter that fits the job ad

A short, honest cover letter in four paragraphs. What to put in, what to leave out, and how to fit it to the job ad.

A cover letter doesn't need to be an essay. A recruiter wants to know in half a minute: why this job, and why you? Four short paragraphs will do it.

Paragraph 1: why this job

Don't open with "I am writing to apply for...". They know. Say in one or two sentences what draws you to this role or this company. Be specific: a product you know, a way of working you like, a step you want to take.

Paragraph 2: what you bring

Pick two requirements from the ad and show with an example that you've already done them. One example each is enough, ideally with something you can measure: how many people, how much time, what it achieved.

At Noordhaven I planned the rosters for two shifts of 40 people. Through peak season we kept the floor fully staffed without extra agency staff.

Paragraph 3: how you work

One or two sentences on how you work with people or solve problems, again with a short example. This is the place for what doesn't fit on your CV.

Paragraph 4: close

Short and friendly. Say you'd be glad to explain more in an interview. No three versions of "I hope to hear from you".

Fitting it to the ad without overdoing it

  • Use the ad's words, but only where they match what you did.
  • Don't repeat your CV. The letter shows why your CV fits this job.
  • Keep it to one page. 250 words that land beat 600 nobody finishes.
  • Read it out loud. Does it sound like you? If not, make it simpler.

Do you always need one?

Not every ad asks for a letter. When they do, or there's a "motivation" field, send one that's really about this job. A generic letter stands out more than no letter, and not in a good way.
